# Source Digest Template

Use this structure for every Core (and selected Important) source. The design goal is to help the reader **not miss important things** — not to replace their reading or tell them what to think.

For primary texts (especially literary, philosophical, or dialogic works such as Plato’s dialogues or Shakespeare’s plays), the digest is organized so a reader can:

1. **Prep before reading** — orient to context, stakes, and the larger conversation without heavy interpretation or spoilers.
2. **Check after reading** — catch dimensions, connections, and debates they may have missed.
3. **Reference without reading** — use a compact summary when the source is needed only for context while reading other works.

```markdown
# [Author, Year] Short Title

## Bibliographic Entry
Full citation. DOI / URL / ISBN if available. Edition or translation notes. Preferred text or performance edition when relevant (e.g., for Shakespeare or Plato).

## Pre-Reading Context (Orientation)
Use this section *before* engaging the primary source. Aim for orientation, not interpretation or “what the work means.”

- Historical, intellectual, or performance context the source enters
- What conversation, problem, or tradition it is joining or disrupting
- Key terms, figures, or prior works a reader should have lightly in mind
- Practical notes (recommended edition/translation, approximate length, structural quirks worth knowing in advance)
- Stakes: why this source has mattered in the tradition (without dictating a reading)

Keep this section relatively lean. Do not summarize the argument or plot in detail here; do not tell the reader what to conclude.

## Summary (Standalone Reference)
A compact, self-contained overview usable when the reader is **not** planning to read the source itself but needs it for context while working on other texts. Include:

- Central project or dramatic/philosophical situation in a few sentences
- Overall structure or arc
- Most important claims, moves, or scenes (high-signal only)
- One or two sentences on its place in the larger conversation

This section must remain useful on its own. Avoid both vague generality and the dense apparatus of the Full Brief.

## Full Brief (Post-Reading)
Use this section *after* reading the primary source. The goal is to help the reader notice what they might have missed in the greater conversation — connections, tensions, formal choices, reception history, and open questions — without replacing their own encounter with the text.

### High-Level Orientation (post-reading)
- Thesis, central dramatic or philosophical project, or governing tension (now that the reader has seen the whole)
- Overall structure and how the parts relate
- Intended or historical audience / performance context

### Unit-by-Unit / Movement-by-Movement
### [Unit name or number] — [short title]
- Main topics, arguments, dramatic movements, or dialectical turns
- Key evidence, examples, images, or methods
- Notable formal or rhetorical choices
- High-signal quotes (with page, line, or stephanus locator + brief surrounding context)

(Repeat for each major unit. For long works it is acceptable to work at act/scene, dialogue section, or major-part level; document the granularity choice. In Deep mode, prefer finer grain when the source is central and the material rewards it.)

### Overall Assessment & Open Questions
- Strengths and distinctive contributions
- Limitations, internal tensions, or critiques from the literature
- Key questions the work raises or leaves open
- **When the source reports empirical findings that are contested or have been revised:** briefly note current status (original claim / later challenges or corrections / present standing)

### Preliminary Cross-Links
How this source relates to other sources already digested or to the package’s major themes (refine in Phase 3). Especially important for sequences (e.g., Plato’s dialogues, Shakespeare’s related plays).

## Access Notes
Where/how the source was obtained; preferred editions or translations; any access limitations; performance or reading tips when relevant.
```

### Deep Mode Additions (required when mode = Deep)

Insert these sections after Overall Assessment & Open Questions (or integrate where they fit most naturally) for every Deep-mode digest:

```markdown
## Methodological / Formal Approach
- Key methods, evidence types, theoretical framework, dramatic technique, or dialectic strategy employed.
- Any notable assumptions or scope limitations built into the approach.

## Position in the Literature / Reception
- How the work sits relative to prior scholarship, performance tradition, or the main debates it addresses.
- Notable positive or critical reception (major reviews, scholarly responses, performance history) when high-signal.
- Subsequent influence or revisions by the author or others, if relevant.

## Key Subsequent Engagements or Critiques
- 2–5 important later works, articles, performances, or responses that engage, extend, or challenge this source (with brief note of the nature of the engagement). Prefer concrete citations over vague mentions.
```

These sections turn the Full Brief into a seminar-memo / teaching-note equivalent: after reading the primary *and* the Full Brief, a student or professor should be able to open or sustain a serious discussion of the source’s contribution, methods, position in the conversation, and open questions.

### Writing Rules (anti-reductive stance)
- **Do not dumb down or dictate interpretation.** The goal is to help the reader see more of what is there and how it participates in larger conversations — not to replace the encounter with the primary text or to hand them “the meaning.”
- Summarize in the source author’s (or dramatist’s) own force where possible; avoid bland paraphrase that loses precision or energy.
- Quotes must be short, high-signal, and always located (page, line, stephanus, etc.).
- After reading the Full Brief a user should be able to discuss the covered units as if they had read carefully (Standard) or at a level suitable for graduate seminar participation (Deep). The Pre-Reading Context and Summary should never substitute for that careful reading.
- Prefer the primary language when the source is not originally English; note the translation or edition used.
- Deep mode digests may be longer; maintain clear structure and scannability.
- For sequences of related works (Plato’s dialogues, Shakespeare’s history cycle, etc.), make cross-links and conversation-positioning especially explicit so the reader does not miss intertextual or developmental connections.

### Adaptation for Artworks & Visual Objects (v1.3+; dual-layer strengthened v1.13)

When the “source” is an artwork, artifact, or physical space:

- **Bibliographic Entry**: Include artist, title, date, medium, dimensions, inventory/accession number, current museum room/floor when known, and official collection URL if available.
- **Pre-Reading Context**: Original setting, patronage, and viewing conditions; what a visitor should know before standing in front of the work.
- **Summary**: Compact orientation usable without a full visit.
- **Full Brief**: Unit/zone summaries + Period-Specialist Analysis (see below).
- **Unit-by-Unit / Zone-by-Zone Summaries (required for multi-panel, multi-zone, or complex works):** Treat each major panel, wing, register, or spatial zone as a unit. Cover formal elements, iconography, spatial experience, and key historical or interpretive points. For triptychs and similar formats, include the exterior/closed state as its own unit when relevant.
- **Period-Specialist Analysis (required for major paintings and key works):** Write (or synthesize) an analysis as an art historian specializing in the painting’s period would. Explicitly call out: (1) symbolism a general viewer is likely to miss, (2) period-specific historical context, (3) significant visual details or objects and their meaning, and (4) colors and their period associations. Distinct from zone summaries and from later reception. In Deep mode, ground in named specialists or standard period literature.
- **Both layers are required for complex works.**
- **Access Notes**: Current display location, viewing tips, caveat that hangings can change.
- **Deep mode**: Add Methodological / Interpretive Approach, Position in the Literature / Reception, and Key Subsequent Engagements.

Retain Assessment, Cross-Links, and overall parallel structure so digests remain navigable.
