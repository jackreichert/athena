# Source Digest Template

Use this exact structure for every Core (and selected Important) source.

```markdown
# [Author, Year] Short Title

## Bibliographic Entry
Full citation. DOI / URL / ISBN if available. Edition or translation notes.

## High-Level Overview
- Thesis or central project of the work
- Overall structure
- Intended audience
- Publication context / significance

## Unit-by-Unit Summaries

### [Unit name or number] — [short title]
- Main topics / arguments advanced
- Key evidence, examples, or methods
- Central takeaways
- Notable quotes (with page or section locator + brief surrounding context)

(Repeat for each major unit. For long books it is acceptable to work at part or major-section level; document the granularity choice. In Deep mode, prefer finer grain when the source is central and the material rewards it.)

## Overall Assessment
- Strengths
- Limitations or critiques (from the literature or evident in the text)
- Key questions the work raises or leaves open
- **When the source reports empirical findings that are contested or have been revised:** briefly note current status (original claim / later challenges or corrections / present standing) so themes can surface Contested Claims accurately.

## Preliminary Cross-Links
How this source relates to other sources already digested (refine in Phase 3).

## Access Notes
Where/how the source was obtained; any access limitations.
```

### Deep Mode Additions (required when mode = Deep)

Insert these sections after Overall Assessment (or integrate where they fit most naturally) for every Deep-mode digest:

```markdown
## Methodological Approach
- Key methods, evidence types, theoretical framework, or analytic strategy employed.
- Any notable assumptions or scope limitations built into the approach.

## Position in the Literature / Reception
- How the work sits relative to prior scholarship or the main debates it addresses.
- Notable positive or critical reception (major reviews, citations, or responses) when known and high-signal.
- Subsequent influence or revisions by the author or others, if relevant.

## Key Subsequent Engagements or Critiques
- 2–5 important later works, articles, or responses that engage, extend, or challenge this source (with brief note of the nature of the engagement). Prefer concrete citations over vague mentions.
```

These sections turn the digest into a seminar-memo equivalent: a reader should be able to open or sustain a graduate seminar discussion of the source’s contribution, methods and assumptions, position in the literature, and place in ongoing debates.

### Writing Rules
- Summarize in the source author’s own voice where possible; avoid bland paraphrase that loses force.
- Quotes must be short, high-signal, and always located.
- After reading the digest a user should be able to discuss the covered units as if they had read the original (Standard) or at a level suitable for graduate seminar participation (Deep).
- Prefer the primary language when the source is not originally English; note the translation used.
- Deep mode digests may be longer; maintain clear structure and scannability.

### Adaptation for Artworks & Visual Objects (v1.3+; period analysis strengthened v1.12)

When the “source” is an artwork, artifact, or physical space:

- **Bibliographic Entry**: Include artist, title, date, medium, dimensions, inventory/accession number, current museum room/floor when known, and official collection URL if available.
- **High-Level Overview**: Subject matter, composition/structure, intended original context, and significance within the collection or art history.
- **Unit-by-Unit / Zone-by-Zone Summaries**: Formal elements (composition, light, color, scale), iconography or symbolism, spatial experience for the viewer, and key historical or interpretive points. Use clear visual locators rather than page numbers.
- **Period-Specialist Analysis (required for major paintings and key works):** Write (or synthesize) an analysis as an art historian specializing in the painting’s period would. Situate the work in the artistic, technical, iconographic, and cultural conventions of its time — style and technique relative to contemporaries, patronage and original function, period-specific meanings and sources, workshop practice where relevant. Explicitly call out: (1) symbolism a general viewer is likely to miss, (2) period-specific historical context that changes how the image reads, (3) significant visual details or objects and their meaning, and (4) colors and their period associations or meanings. This is distinct from generic formal description and from later reception history. In Deep mode, prefer grounding in named period specialists or standard period literature and note major interpretive debates within that specialist literature.
- **Access Notes**: Emphasize current display location, any viewing tips (best approach angle, lighting, crowd timing), and the caveat that hangings can change.
- **Deep mode**: Add Methodological / Interpretive Approach, Position in the Literature / Reception (key scholarly interpretations, including period-specialist views), and Key Subsequent Engagements (major critical or curatorial responses).

Retain the overall Assessment, Cross-Links, and structure so digests remain parallel and navigable.
