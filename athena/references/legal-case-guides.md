# Legal Case Deep Dives / Case Method (Athena Adaptive Application)

Load this file when the topic is a judicial opinion or set of opinions, case briefing, the case method, Socratic classroom analysis, or an explicit request for guidance on reading / briefing / analyzing legal cases. Orthogonal to package shape (Survey / Standard / Deep).

This adaptive application is grounded in the Discovery-first package on the pedagogy of the legal case method (Langdellian origins, case-briefing practice, Socratic dynamics, critiques, and modern contextual / problem-solving evolutions).

## Core orientation
Athena legal-case packages treat the judicial opinion (or problem-solving case study) as the primary object of study. The goal is to help the reader:
- Isolate legally relevant facts and procedural posture
- Formulate precise issues
- Extract and state the governing rule or principle
- Reconstruct the court’s application and reasoning
- Evaluate the holding, its scope, and its relationship to prior and subsequent authority
- Recognize what the opinion leaves out (context, alternative narratives, policy stakes) when the package shape and sources support it
- Practice the generative moves expected in Socratic discussion and on exams (hypotheticals, distinctions, policy arguments)

Digests and themes should support both careful individual briefing and productive participation in case-method classrooms or journal-style discussions.

## Source treatment
- The focal judicial opinion(s) are Core sources.
- Key prior or subsequent cases that define the doctrine, leading secondary treatments, and high-quality pedagogical materials may be ranked Important or Supplementary.
- When the request is meta (how the case method or briefing works), the historical and practical pedagogy sources themselves become Core.

## Digest adaptations for judicial opinions
Use the standard three-part structure (Pre-Reading Context / Summary / Full Brief) with the following domain-specific emphases. The Full Brief should be usable both as a model brief and as a richer seminar note.

**Pre-Reading Context**
- Doctrinal area and why this case is taught
- Key prior authority or controversy a reader should have lightly in mind
- Practical notes (length, density, whether the opinion is heavily edited in the casebook, presence of important dissents or concurrences)
- What kind of work the case is doing (establishing a rule, refining an exception, resolving a circuit split, illustrating a policy tension, etc.)

**Summary (Standalone)**
- Parties and core dispute in one or two sentences
- Procedural posture
- Precise issue(s)
- Holding and disposition
- One-sentence statement of the rule or principle the case is taken to stand for

**Full Brief (Post-Reading)** — denser and more analytic
- Legally relevant facts (and which facts the court treats as material)
- Procedural history
- Issue(s) formulated with precision
- Rule(s) stated cleanly (black-letter or principle)
- Court’s application / reasoning, including how it handles counter-arguments, distinctions, or policy
- Holding and disposition
- Scope notes: what the case clearly decides, what it leaves open, how later cases have treated it (when known)
- Alternative readings or critiques that appear in the pedagogical or secondary literature (especially in Deep packages)
- Generative close: productive hypotheticals, distinctions, or policy questions the case invites

**Deep-mode additions**
- Methodological / formal approach (how the court reasons)
- Position in the doctrine and subsequent reception
- Explicit attention to what narratives or stakes the majority opinion omits, when the sources support it

For multi-case sequences (classic casebook clusters), produce linked digests and a short doctrinal synthesis note.

## Theme emphases
Recurring high-value themes for legal-case packages:
- Issue formulation and framing
- Rule extraction and statement
- Fact selection and materiality
- Application and analogical reasoning
- Holding vs. dicta; scope of the decision
- Relationship to prior and subsequent authority
- Policy, institutional, or critical perspectives that the opinion engages or suppresses
- (When relevant) Differences between classic appellate case method and problem-solving case-study approaches

Seminar Guiding Questions (Deep) should progress from accurate reconstruction of the opinion → critique of framing or reasoning → comparison across cases → design of a hypothetical or alternative argument.

## Active learning & deliverables
- Case-brief templates and worked examples are natural deliverables.
- Socratic-style and hypothetical-driven discussion questions are preferred.
- Optional short exercises: “re-brief focusing only on the dissent’s framing,” “write the narrowest and broadest plausible holdings,” “draft the next hypothetical the professor is likely to ask,” “map how later cases have narrowed or extended this one.”
- For Deep packages, include a brief note on how an instructor might use the package in a real case-method class or academic-support session.

## Relationship to shared spine
All standard Athena rules remain in force (provenance, ranked sources, citation density in themes, further-reading pathways, anti-reductive stance). This file supplies only the domain overlays for judicial opinions and case-method pedagogy.
